About

I’m a fourth-year undergraduate at KAIST, double-majoring in Computer Science and Industrial & Systems Engineering.

Research interests

I’m interested in machine learning, reinforcement learning and operations research, and especially in where the three meet. A lot of real-world decisions, like scheduling a production line, managing inventory or deciding which companies should share a shipment, are too big to solve exactly, and simple rules of thumb often aren’t good enough. I like working on problems like these, where learning can make a real difference.

What fascinates me most is how learning agents actually get better at making decisions: how their behavior changes during training, why some of them generalize to new situations while others don’t, and how they sometimes find strategies people wouldn’t have designed by hand. I’m just as curious about the opposite question, when learning isn’t needed at all and a simpler method does the job.

Right now I’m exploring this at COMET Lab with Prof. Changhyun Kwon, working on fair cost sharing for companies that ship goods together. I’d love to keep building methods that are both well understood and useful in practice, in industry and beyond.
